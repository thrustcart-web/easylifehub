A seed starting tray lets you grow seedlings indoors; choose one that fits your space, offers reliable drainage, and can be reused for multiple seasons, saving time and resources while giving you control over germination conditions.

Seed starting trays simplify the early growth stage by keeping seeds at a stable temperature and moisture level. They also let you organize varieties, label rows, and move seedlings to larger containers once they develop true leaves.

This guide reviews the Burpee SuperSeed 36‑Cell reusable tray, covering drainage, reusability, cell capacity, setup, and upkeep. Follow the sections to verify each aspect against your gardening setup before making a purchase.

## Does the tray provide adequate drainage?

Effective drainage prevents waterlogging and root rot.

The Burpee SuperSeed 36‑Cell tray lists drainage as a feature, so confirm that the cells have holes or a built‑in channel and that the tray fits your preferred watering method.

## Is the tray reusable and durable?

Reusability reduces waste and saves money over multiple growing cycles.

This Burpee model is marketed as reusable, so check the material—often a sturdy plastic—and verify that it can withstand repeated washing without cracking.

## How many cells do you need for your planting plan?

Cell count determines how many seedlings you can start at once.

The SuperSeed tray offers 36 cells, which works well for small to medium seed batches, but larger gardeners may prefer trays with 72 or more cells and should verify the dimensions to ensure they fit their growing space.

## What setup steps are required before planting?

Proper setup ensures consistent germination.

Place the tray on a flat surface, add a moisture‑retaining medium, and confirm the drainage holes are unobstructed; the product description does not detail a specific liner, so you may need a separate humidity dome.

## How should you maintain the tray between uses?

Cleaning prevents disease carryover.

After each cycle, rinse the tray with water, allow it to dry completely, and inspect for cracks; because the listing does not specify a cleaning recommendation, follow standard horticultural practices for plastic seed trays.

## Conclusion

Choosing a seed starting tray hinges on matching drainage, reusability, and cell count to your indoor growing conditions and seasonal plans.
Verify the tray’s material, confirm the drainage design, and ensure it fits your space and watering style before buying, and you’ll have a reliable foundation for healthy seedlings.
